About

I’m Akash Mishra, a software engineer and an engineering manager with a soft spot for products. I was a product engineer long before AI made the term fashionable. I live in Bangalore, India.

These days I’m head of product engineering for the ecommerce business unit at Brevo, where I work on making marketing easier for ecommerce operators.

In my personal time, I read a lot (books, Substack, X) and write a bit here and there. I sign up for marathons and triathlons and then start training for them. I somehow managed to finish Ironman 70.3 Goa 2026 in 8:15.

I go by tucosaurus most places online.

How I got here

I started writing software in college, mostly by hanging around the local Python and Linux communities, and helped organise PyCon India in 2016 and 2017. The work and the people there taught me the shape of real-world software development.

My first job was at Betaout, while I was still in college. I was part of a small team modernising their architecture and making it scale. I drank from the firehose and worked across the stack: backend APIs, frontend, and devops. I lost count of how many times I broke production. It was the best of times, it was the worst of times.

Next, I spent three and a half years building apps at Fueled. That taught me how to ship user-facing products, how to think about MVPs, and what good user experience looks like.

In 2021 I joined PushOwl, a small and mighty team building a Shopify app for ecommerce marketing. Later that year Sendinblue (now Brevo) acquired PushOwl, and I came along with it. I played a small part in rebuilding the engineering team and getting the business growing again by launching two new products.

These were the years that changed me most. I was in a place where there was no right answer and the way forward had to come from me. They taught me how contextual each statement out there on the internet is and how to think through problems for myself.

In 2026, the PushOwl team merged into the wider Brevo organisation. I went from leading a team of 12 to 30+ people. The problems got bigger and messier. Some are about product, some about the business and most are about people. Now, I am figuring out how to keep the speed of a small team inside a big one.
